Time Tracking Pricing Comparison 2027

General
26 Nov 2026
Valentina
@ivalentina

Are you a solopreneur, business owner or manager of a large distributed team looking for clear, up-to-date time tracking pricing comparisons? The right tool can make a big difference to your productivity and your budget. Below you’ll find ten popular time tracking apps, with their current prices, features and core differences, so you can quickly decide which one fits your workflow. We start with Harvest, the app whose pricing changed the most in 2026.

Top 10 Time Tracking apps to use in 2027

Harvest: things got complicated

Harvest is one of the popular time tracking apps known for built-in invoicing and expense tracking, which makes it popular for example with contractors and agencies that bill clients for tracked time. In 2026, though, most people searching for Harvest are asking about its price increase and what Harvest actually costs now.

What changed in Harvest pricing? After Bending Spoons bought Harvest in 2025, it replaced flat per-seat pricing with two paid plans, Teams and Enterprise. Both now charge a per-seat price plus usage fees once you exceed an allowance (Flex billing), or a higher flat seat price (Unlimited billing). The BBC reported renewals rising by up to 1,500%.

So how much does Harvest cost now? The Teams plan starts at €8.20 per seat/month billed annually, and Enterprise at €12.80 before usage fees.

The free plan includes: 1 seat and 2 projects.

Clockify: the free plan now stops at five users

Clockify is well known for its free plan, but since 2026 that plan covers only up to five users, and several features that used to be free are now in paid tiers. Paid seat prices haven’t changed: Basic starts at €3.99 and Standard at €5.49 per seat/month billed annually. Paid tiers unlock features such as timesheet approvals, billable rates, advanced reporting and project templates. It’s a good fit for freelancers and companies that want lightweight tracking that scales.

Cheapest paid plan includes: timesheets, reporting, billable rates, project management controls.

Toggl Track: now billed per license

Toggl Track focuses on simplicity and attractive, well-designed dashboards. Its free plan makes it popular, but you should also think about the hidden costs of free software. What happens to your data and your privacy, for example? In 2026 Toggl started billing per license instead of per user and raised its monthly rates. Starter is still €9 per license/month billed annually.

Cheapest paid plan includes: unlimited tracking, billable rates, project templates, basic reporting.

Hubstaff: tracking with monitoring features

Hubstaff includes activity monitoring, screenshots, automatic payroll and GPS tracking. That makes it a good fit for field teams and businesses that need more accountability. The Starter plan still costs €4.99 per user/month billed annually.

Cheapest paid plan includes: time tracking, activity monitoring, screenshots, basic reports.

TimeTrack: time and attendance

TimeTrack focuses on digital time clocks, staff attendance, breaks and vacation tracking. It’s widely used in the DACH region for mandatory working time records. The cheapest plan costs €5 per user/month billed annually.

Cheapest paid plan includes: attendance tracking, breaks, absences, timesheets.

clockin: simple time tracking for on-site teams

clockin is built for trades, on-site workers and field teams that need a straightforward punch-in / punch-out system. In 2026 it moved to three plans: Starter at €3.90, Pro at €6.70 and Expert at €13.50 per user/month billed annually. Project time tracking starts with Pro. Shift planning is now a paid add-on, from €27 per month.

Cheapest paid plan includes: punch clock, mobile tracking, absence management, digital personnel file, DATEV interface.

Clockodo: time tracking made in Germany, now cheaper to start

Clockodo offers precise time tracking, invoicing exports and strong reporting. It’s especially popular with small and medium businesses in German-speaking markets. Clockodo lowered its entry price in 2026. Basic costs €3.80 per user/month billed annually, and covers working time and absences. Project time tracking is in Pro, at €9.50 per user/month (billed annually).

Cheapest paid plan includes: working time tracking, absence management, mobile apps, reports.

Factorial: HR suite with time tracking included

Factorial isn’t only a time tracking tool. It’s a full HR platform, and time tracking is one of many modules. Prices start at €8 per user/month.

Cheapest paid plan includes: HR core tools, basic time and attendance features.

Zeitguru: affordable time tracking and payroll exports

Zeitguru offers one of the lowest per-user prices, at €3.50 per user/month, and includes shift planning, attendance and payroll-ready exports. There is a minimum fee of €49 per month, which is the price of 14 users.

Cheapest paid plan includes: time clock, shift planning, vacation management, payroll exports.

Kimai: open source, privacy-focused, no hidden costs

Kimai is a fully featured time tracking solution built on open source. It has a powerful cloud version and a self-hosted free version used worldwide. Pricing stays transparent and flat: from €2.99 per user/month billed annually, with no usage fees. Kimai also has an offer for non-profits: €0.99 per user/month.

Cheapest paid plan includes: project tracking, billable and non-billable hours, invoicing, reports, exports, etc.

Final thoughts

A low per-user price means reliable value only when the pricing is transparent and the discounts are simple. Otherwise, the pricing page is only a starting point.

If you’re seriously considering a new time tracking software for your team, read the small print carefully. Look for minimum fees, required license counts, long contract terms, usage-based charges and first-year prices that renew higher. Check what you’ll pay in the second year, not only in the first month.

The simpler the pricing model, the easier it is to plan your budget and grow your team without surprises. By the way, at Kimai we keep our pricing fair and never hide a higher price in the small print.
