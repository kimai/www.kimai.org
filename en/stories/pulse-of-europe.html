Pulse of Europe

Head of Office: Hendrik Michel
Industry: Civic movement
Employees: 3+
Using Kimai since: 2026
Homepage: www.pulseofeurope.eu
Location: Frankfurt, Germany

How a European non-profit uses Kimai to show where its time goes

TL;DR

Pulse of Europe is a non-partisan civic movement whose Frankfurt office coordinates a network of volunteer groups across Europe. For nine years, the team logged its hours in Excel – which worked fine until they needed to see time by project. Now the office tracks its time in Kimai against a shared project structure and can show exactly how much effort goes into the mission and how much goes into admin.

About the company

Pulse of Europe is a non-partisan, independent civic movement founded in Frankfurt am Main in 2016. It campaigns for European integration and core democratic values. The movement is a network of volunteers from all over Europe.

Savings with Kimai

  • Faster time tracking than with the old Excel spreadsheets
  • Easier reporting on project and admin time, with export built in
  • One shared structure with consistent project names means less upkeep
  • The dashboard makes it easy to spot overtime and quieter periods and plan work around them

Project time tracking usually brings to mind billable hours, budgets and profit margins. But even when the goal is a cause rather than a profit, time is still a limited resource.

That’s why Hendrik Michel, Head of Office at Pulse of Europe, expects more from time tracking than a record of when people start and finish work. The movement wants to see how much of its work goes into projects and how much goes into administration. So this year, the office started using Kimai’s project-based time tracking.

“For us, this has nothing to do with keeping tabs on people,” Hendrik explains. What really matters is “how much time goes into our projects – in other words, into our actual mission – and how much goes into admin.” Non-profit or for-profit, everyone needs to know where their time is going.

From spreadsheets to a shared project structure

For nine years, Pulse of Europe tracked working hours in spreadsheets. The sheet calculated overtime and accounted for vacation, public holidays and sick days. For that, it did the job. Things got trickier when the team also wanted to track and report on time spent on individual projects.

“It was a good Excel tool, but it had its limits,” Hendrik recalls. Tracking time by project meant everyone needed the same project names and a structure they could all work with. That’s where the old system hit a wall: it took more and more effort to maintain, and the data couldn’t always be analyzed consistently.

Keeping the big picture is especially hard when you’re growing fast across borders: “We’re very decentralized. Our office is in Frankfurt, and everyone else is organized into local groups that are active in Germany and many other European countries and volunteer their time for Europe.”

In Kimai, the office set up top-level areas such as administration and mission work, with the individual projects underneath. Whether it’s citizen engagement or a campaign, all the time ends up in the same structure. “We all work with the same projects and tools. That was really important to us, too,” says Hendrik. The real win wasn’t just going digital – it was that the entries can now be compared and analyzed.

How hard was the switch to Kimai?

The team deliberately took its time. “We ran Kimai alongside the old system for three months,” says Hendrik. “That gave us time to get everything ready and import the data later on.” During that period, staff could set up projects, agree on naming and figure out whether they preferred the stopwatch or logging time in 15-minute blocks.

Switching mid-year wasn’t a problem either – according to Hendrik, the existing data imported without a hitch. By the time the team made the final switch, the big questions had been settled and everyone knew their way around the system: “Kimai became part of our daily routine right away.”

Kimai makes everything so much easier and more pleasant – it’s faster, and it’s more fun

Hendrik Michel
Head of Office – Pulse of Europe

Showing what’s behind administrative costs

At the general meeting, the association has to account for how it used its resources. Hendrik sums up the value of Kimai’s reports like this: “The reports let us break down in great detail how much time we spent on a particular project.” They show how many hours went into citizen engagement, campaigns and other projects – and how much went into admin.

This isn’t about clearing up any mistrust. “The trust is there, and nobody questions it. But now we can also show exactly what we do,” Hendrik stresses. The time records also help the association document its charitable work for the tax office.

Everyone needs time tracking – non-profits just as much as businesses

A business wants to know whether its projects pay off. A non-profit wants to show how its resources serve its mission. The goals are different, but the underlying question is the same: where does the time go?

So time tracking isn’t just for companies that bill by the hour. It matters to anyone who’s accountable for time, money and results. To keep professional time tracking affordable for non-profits on a tight budget, Kimai offers a discounted NPO plan for its cloud service.

Kimai gave Pulse of Europe the common ground it needed: easy tracking, consistent projects and reports that clearly show the work that’s been done.

A big thank you to Hendrik Michel for the inspiring conversation, and all the best to Pulse of Europe for the future!
